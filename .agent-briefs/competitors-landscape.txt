TASK: Map Posta's competitor landscape and find the gaps we can own with a South African ("Mzansi") twist.

CONTEXT: Posta is a South African AI social-media publishing product for small businesses and creators.
It writes posts in all 11 official SA languages, schedules them, and publishes to Instagram, Facebook, X and
LinkedIn. Billing is Paystack in rands. It is pre-launch. We need to know precisely what the competitors
have, what they charge, and where a local product can win rather than copy.

WHAT TO PRODUCE: a single markdown file at /root/posta-revive/docs/COMPETITIVE-LANDSCAPE.md

SECTIONS THE FILE MUST CONTAIN:

1. Coverage table. One row per competitor, columns: product, who it is really for, free-tier limit,
   entry paid price (state the currency AND the converted rand figure with the rate you used and when),
   number of platforms supported, whether it does AI generation, whether it supports any African language,
   whether it supports WhatsApp.
   Cover at least: Buffer, Hootsuite, Later, Metricool, Publer, SocialBee, Vista Social, FeedHive, Postiz.

2. What each one actually does well - the one or two things they beat us on today. Be specific, not
   "good UX". Example of the right level: "Publer lets one plan hold 3 brands with per-brand approval
   workflows" or "Metricool includes competitor tracking and a shareable client report".

3. Pricing reality for a South African buyer. Card-in-rands matters: note which of them bill in USD only
   and what that means for a Joburg salon paying with a rand card (declined cards, forex fees, no local
   support). State the rand equivalent using a rate you looked up, and say where you got it.

4. THE GAP LIST - the most important section. 8 to 12 concrete gaps, each with:
   - the gap in one line
   - who has it and who does not (evidence, with a URL)
   - how Posta could take it with a Mzansi angle
   - a rough effort size (small / medium / large)
   Rank them by how hard they are for a competitor to copy, easiest-to-copy last.
   Think about things global tools do not do and will not: 11 official languages actually proofread rather
   than machine-translated, load-shedding tolerant scheduled posting (the phone is off, the laptop is off,
   the post still must go), pay-as-you-go prepaid credits in rands with no card required, WhatsApp as a
   channel and as the notification route, township and spaza-shop sized businesses with one phone and no
   laptop, public holidays and local calendar moments, POPIA-shaped data handling, offline-first drafts.

5. Five features we should build next, in order, each justified by a numbered gap from section 4.

EVIDENCE RULES - these are not negotiable:
- Every price, limit and feature claim needs the URL you read it on. No URL, no claim.
- If you could not verify something, write "not verified" next to it. Never guess a price.
- Marketing pages overstate. Where a pricing page and a help-centre article disagree, say so.
- Do not review-bomb: no invented quotes, no invented market size numbers, no "studies show". We are the
  ones who get hurt by invented data.
- Today's date is 24 October 2026. Anything older than about 6 months should be flagged as possibly stale.

TOOLS: web search and page reads. Reading a public pricing page is fine. Do not sign up for trials, do not
enter card details, do not log in to anything. No purchases.

HARD RULES:
- Write only the one markdown file. Do not modify any code.
- No git state changes.
- Do not restart or kill anything on port 3008.

REPORT BACK: the file path, the number of competitors covered, the top 5 gaps in one line each, and
anything you could not verify.
